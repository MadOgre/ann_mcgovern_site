About Ann McGovern

AM

Ann McGovern
1930–2018

Ann McGovern (May 25, 1930 – October 6, 2018) was a beloved American children's book author who wrote more than 50 books over a remarkable career. Her books have been translated into many languages and are used in classrooms and libraries around the world.

Born in New York City, Ann developed a love of reading and storytelling at an early age. She went on to become one of the most prolific and celebrated children's authors of her generation, writing everything from folktales and picture books to nonfiction science books and biographies of remarkable historical figures.

Biography

Ann McGovern grew up in New York City, where her passion for books and learning took root. She attended the High School of Music & Art and later worked as an editor before turning her full attention to writing for children. Her debut book, Aesop's Fables, was published in 1963, launching a literary career that would span more than five decades.

Ann had an extraordinary gift for making history accessible to young readers. Her landmark "If You Lived..." series brought the past vividly to life, allowing children to imagine what it would be like to live during the time of the Sioux Indians, in Colonial America, or during the Civil Rights movement. These books became staples of elementary school curricula across the United States.

Her Too Much Noise (1967) — a retelling of a classic Jewish folktale about a farmer who learns to appreciate the quiet of his home — became one of the most enduringly popular picture books for early readers. With its repetitive, cumulative structure and gentle humor, the story has delighted generations of children and their parents.

"Children are curious. They want to know how things work and why. My job is to feed that curiosity and inspire them to ask more questions." — Ann McGovern

Scuba Diver & Ocean Adventurer

One of the most remarkable aspects of Ann's life was her passion for scuba diving. She became a certified diver and traveled the world's oceans, descending to coral reefs, exploring shipwrecks, and swimming alongside sharks — and then writing about it all for children.

Her ocean books, including The Underwater World of the Coral Reef (1976), Shark Lady: True Adventures of Eugenie Clark (1978), and Diving to the Past: Recovering Ancient Wrecks (1990), brought the wonders of the deep sea to young readers in vivid, accessible prose. Shark Lady in particular has enjoyed a remarkable renaissance in recent years, introducing a new generation to the pioneering marine biologist Eugenie Clark and the importance of protecting sharks.

Ann logged hundreds of dives over her lifetime, exploring sites in the Caribbean, the Pacific, and the Red Sea. She was a firm believer that firsthand experience was the best research, and her underwater adventures gave her books an authenticity that set them apart.

Awards & Recognition

Ann McGovern's books have received numerous honors throughout the years:

  • Too Much Noise — Named an American Library Association Notable Book
  • Shark Lady — A Children's Choice Award winner and consistently named to "best of" lists
  • Wanted Dead or Alive: The True Story of Harriet Tubman — Widely adopted in elementary school curricula
  • The Secret Soldier — Recognized by the National Council for Social Studies
  • The "If You Lived..." series — Used in thousands of classrooms throughout North America
  • Multiple "Teacher's Pick" and "Parent's Choice" recognitions

Legacy

Ann McGovern passed away on October 6, 2018, at the age of 88. She left behind a legacy of curiosity, adventure, and the unwavering belief that children deserve to know the truth about history, science, and the world around them — told with honesty, warmth, and a dash of wonder.

Her books continue to be read, taught, and treasured in homes, schools, and libraries. Too Much Noise remains in print after more than 50 years. Shark Lady has experienced a remarkable resurgence, becoming a bestseller once again. The "If You Lived..." series continues to educate new generations about American history.

Ann McGovern believed deeply in the power of books to change lives — and in that, she was undeniably right.
